Can Foreigners Buy Property in Panama? Steps, Risks and Official Sources

[DIRECT ANSWER – AEO, 55 words]
Yes, foreigners can generally buy property in Panama, with restrictions in specific areas such as border zones and islands. The usual steps are to vet the developer and the title, have a Panamanian attorney review the contract, confirm how payments are protected, sign the public deed and register it. Confirm your case with an attorney.

NOTICE (visible): This guide is informational and is not legal or tax advice. Rules and amounts change. Verify each point with a licensed Panamanian attorney and the official agency.

## The general rule, and its exceptions

In general, foreigners can buy real estate in Panama. There are restrictions in specific zones, for example border areas and islands, and the status of land near the shoreline needs confirmation. Your attorney should check that the property is not in a restricted zone, that its legal status is clear and that there are no liens. ## Step 1: Set a budget that includes all costs

Decide whether you will live in the property, rent it or resell it. Add closing costs, monthly fees, insurance and taxes. Use current amounts from the official sources, not examples. [COMPLETE: links to the current DGI and Registro Público pages.]

## Step 2: Vet the developer and the project

Ask for: the developer's legal entity and representative, the land title free of liens (your attorney checks at the Registro Público), construction permits, and a record of delivered projects you can visit. See our checklist, [How to verify a real estate developer in Panama] (link to /como-verificar-una-promotora-panama/, Spanish).

## Step 3: Have an attorney review the purchase agreement

Check: the exact unit (area, plan, parking), the delivery date and what happens if it slips, what changes the developer may make, what happens if you withdraw or the developer defaults, and who pays each closing cost.
