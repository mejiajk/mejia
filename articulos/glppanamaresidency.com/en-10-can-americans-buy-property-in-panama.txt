Can Americans Buy Property in Panama? Steps, Risks and Official Sources

[DIRECT ANSWER – AEO, 56 words]
Americans can generally buy property in Panama, with restrictions in specific zones. The usual steps are to vet the seller and the title, have a Panamanian attorney review the contract, confirm how payments are protected, sign the public deed and register it. Buying property is a separate matter from residency. Confirm your case with an attorney.

## The general rule and its exceptions

In general, foreigners, including Americans, can buy real estate in Panama. There are restrictions in specific zones, for example border areas and islands, and the status of land near the shoreline needs confirmation. Your attorney must confirm the property is not in a restricted zone and has no liens. [VERIFY with the legal reviewer; link the current official source.]

## Buying property is not the same as getting residency

Buying a property does not by itself grant residency. Some residency routes involve an investment and others do not. Read [Qualified Investor vs Friendly Nations vs Pensionado] (link to /en/qualified-investor-vs-friendly-nations-vs-pensionado/).

## Step 1: Set your goal and budget

Decide whether you will live in the property, rent it or resell it. Add closing costs, fees, insurance and taxes to the price. Use current amounts from official sources and your attorney, not examples. [COMPLETE: links to the current DGI and Registro Público pages.]

## Step 2: Vet the seller and the project

Ask for the seller's legal entity, the land title free of liens (your attorney checks at the Registro Público), construction permits for new projects and a record of delivered work.

## Step 3: Have your attorney review the contract

Check the exact property, the delivery date and delay terms, what changes the seller may make, what happens if you withdraw or the seller defaults, and who pays each closing cost.

## Step 4: Confirm how payments are protected

Ask in writing where payments are held and under what guarantee. Do not pay into accounts the contract does not name.

## Step 6: Public deed and registration

A public deed is signed and recorded at the Registro Público. Your attorney coordinates the taxes and fees currently due. [COMPLETE: taxes and fees with the current figure, official source and date. Do not publish percentages without that source.]

## Step 7: Handover and ongoing costs

Inspect the property against the contract and put every observation in writing. Plan for ongoing costs such as maintenance, insurance and property taxes. [COMPLETE with official sources.]

## Risks to consider

- Buying without independent legal advice.
- Paying into accounts the contract does not mention.
- Assuming a purchase gives residency or tax benefits.
- Expecting income or appreciation without documented basis.
